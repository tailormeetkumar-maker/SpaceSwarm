AUTONOMOUS SPACE EXPLORATION · RESEARCH PROTOTYPE

One Mother.
Many Bees.
One Swarm.

A mission concept where a Mother spacecraft carries a swarm of small autonomous explorers. The Mother reaches the exploration zone, releases the Bees, and the swarm explores, communicates, learns and recovers together.

Start the mission tour ↓
01 · THE PROBLEM

Space exploration is expensive, distant and uncertain.

A single explorer can become a single point of failure. SpaceSwarm investigates a distributed approach: many smaller autonomous agents cooperate instead of depending on one vehicle for every exploration task.

Single failure

One failed explorer can remove a large part of the mission capability.

Limited coverage

One spacecraft cannot investigate many locations simultaneously.

Communication delay

Earth cannot continuously control every small decision in a distant environment.

Knowledge loss

A failed agent should not erase the hive's collective knowledge.

02 · LAUNCH

The mission begins with the Mother.

Rocket launch

A launch vehicle carries the Mother spacecraft and its stored Bees into space.

Transit

The Mother travels toward the exploration region. Propulsion and orbital mechanics are abstracted in the current simulation.

Arrival

The Mother establishes the hive's operating area and becomes its communication gateway.

Bee deployment

The Mother releases the autonomous Bees. They begin exploring instead of waiting for individual Earth instructions.

05 · PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS

The swarm is designed around failure.

Mother is too far away

The Bee cannot deliver directly.

→ Relay

Nearby same-hive Bees forward the message.

A Bee fails

A route or capability can disappear.

→ Recovery

The Mother detects losses and reallocates roles or deploys a replacement.

Duplicate messages

The same discovery may travel more than once.

→ Deduplication

Observation/message IDs prevent repeated processing.

Messages could circulate forever

Uncontrolled forwarding wastes communication.

→ TTL + hop limits

Messages have bounded forwarding lifetimes.

06 · FAILURE & RECOVERY

The hive does not simply start over.

Failure

A Bee becomes unavailable in the simulation.

Detection

The Mother tracks hive membership and health.

Capability check

The hive checks whether important roles are missing.

Role reallocation

Remaining Bees can receive updated roles.

Replacement

When the configured threshold is reached, a new Bee can join.

Knowledge inheritance

The replacement can inherit hive-level knowledge maintained by the Mother.

07 · THE COMMUNICATION HIERARCHY

Earth does not micromanage the swarm.

Bee ↔ Bee

Local cooperation inside one hive.

Bee ↔ Mother

Hive-level coordination and information delivery.

Mother ↔ Earth

High-level reports and commands.

This lets local decisions happen close to the exploration environment while Earth remains the high-level mission layer.
